Quick history of Arunachal Pradesh

Location: Northeastern India — Eastern Himalayas

Arunachal Pradesh — literally "land of the rising sun" — is home to many indigenous tribes with rich cultural traditions and diverse languages. It became a union territory of India after independence and was granted full statehood in 1987. The state features large tracts of forest, high mountain passes (like Sela), and important biodiversity hotspots such as Namdapha National Park.

Because of its steep terrain, intense monsoon rains, and seismic activity being on the Himalayan thrust systems, the state is naturally prone to floods, landslides and frequent low-to-moderate earthquakes.

Recent natural disasters (summary)

The list below highlights notable events in recent monsoon seasons and seismic activity.

  • June 2025 — Heavy monsoon rainfall, floods & landslides
    Many districts affected; fatalities and thousands displaced in early June 2025.
  • Late May — landslide in East Kameng (May 30, 2025)
    Multiple deaths reported when a vehicle was swept away and other landslides damaged property in Bana–Seppa area.
  • Cloudburst / flash floods in Itanagar (2024-2025 seasons)
    Sudden cloudbursts have caused flash floods inundating parts of the Itanagar region and damaging roads and houses.
  • Frequent small earthquakes (2023-2025)
    Multiple small-magnitude quakes recorded in districts such as East Kameng and West Kameng; shallow quakes occasionally felt but often cause little damage.

Detailed timeline & preparedness tips

  1. May–June 2025 monsoon crisis — intense rains triggered flash floods across many districts, with landslides reported in East Kameng, Dibang Valley, Anjaw and others. Relief operations and air-drops were used to help cut-off villages.
  2. Common impacts — damaged roads, washed-away bridges, destroyed crops, temporary sheltering in relief camps, and travel disruption along NH routes (including sections of NH-13 / BCT highway).
  3. Preparedness tips — keep an emergency bag (food, water, torch, first-aid), register with local disaster authorities, avoid river banks during heavy rain, and follow official evacuation orders.
  4. How you can help — support verified local relief funds/NGOs, donate through government-endorsed channels, or volunteer through district disaster management teams.
